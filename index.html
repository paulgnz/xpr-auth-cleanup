🧹 XPR Auth Cleanup

Audit your XPR Network account for malicious permissions and remove them in a single transaction you sign yourself.

⚠️ What's going on

A phishing campaign (e.g. xprdrop.com) tricks XPR Network users into signing away control of their account. It's a permission-delegation drainer — no private key is stolen; you authorize it yourself by signing what looks like an airdrop "claim".

You receive an unsolicited token (e.g. XPRDROP) whose memo advertises an airdrop site.
On the site, "claiming" makes you sign a transaction that creates a new permission (usually named claim) controlled by the attacker's account, and links it to your token transfers — and sometimes your unstaking.
Seconds later the attacker uses that permission to transfer out your tokens. Because it's a permission, not a one-off transfer, it persists — they can drain you again when new funds arrive, until you delete it.
Am I affected? / How this tool helps

Connect your wallet below (or type any account name for a read-only audit) and this tool inspects every permission on the account and every linked action, flagging anything controlled by an account that isn't you. If it finds the malicious permission, it builds one transaction — unlinkauth for each linked action, then deleteauth — for you to sign. It never sees your keys, has no backend, and can only ever remove permissions — it cannot move funds.

Trust check. This tool is deliberately the mirror image of the scam: it asks you to connect and sign an auth transaction — but only to remove access, never grant it. Every action is shown in full before you sign. Source is one readable file: view on GitHub. If you hold staked XPR, clean up FIRST, then unstake (explained after the audit).
